Disk Array or Logical Drive

What is the difference between a "Disk Array" and a "Logical Drive" and why do they both appear in ?

A disk array is a collection of physical (disk) drives working together in a RAID. A logical drive is an entity that the computer's operating system recognizes as single drive. This single drive is called a "logical" drive, to distinguish it from the multiple physical drives in the the disk array that supports it. Another reason for the name is because the "logical" drive is the creation of the RAID management software's "logic". Depending on how you have managed its capacity, you can create multiple logical drives from a single disk array.

Create Disk Array/Logical Drive

Why can't I see all RAID Levels in RAID Level dropdown menu?

The selection of RAID Levels shown depends on number of free physical drives available to the controller. For example, if there are only two physical drives free, then you cannot see RAID 10, which requires four physical drives or RAID 50 that requires at least six. See RAID Levels.

When I try to click the Submit button, why is it disabled?

The Submit button only enables when you make a change. After you click it once, it disables again, confirming that your selection was submitted.

Why can't I create more than one logical drive on my disk array?

Your logical drive takes up the entire configurable capacity of your disk array, leaving no room for another logical drive. Backup your important data, then delete the existing logical drive and create multiple smaller logical drives on the disk array.

Disk Array Degraded

According to , my disk array is degraded. What am I supposed to do?

If the Auto Rebuild option is enabled and a hot spare drive is available, your disk array will begin rebuilding automatically. See Logical Drive Degraded.

When an disk array becomes degraded, can I still access the data on it?

Yes, but reads and writes will take longer while rebuilding is in progress.

Delete Disk Array/Logical Drive

Why can't I select Delete Disk Array or Delete Logical Drive?

You must have Power or Super User Rights to delete a Logical Drive. See your Administrator about upgrading your access rights.
If there is no Disk Array or Logical Drive under the controller, the Delete does not function.

Can I delete a Logical Drive without deleting the Disk Array?

Yes. Click on a disk array in Tree View then click on the Delete LD tab.
Important: Remember to backup any important data before you delete the logical drive.

Rebuild Disk Array

I replaced a failed physical drive with a used but known-good disk drive. will not rebuild to it. Why not?

The replacement drive was previously used in a different disk array/logical drive. You must wipe (erase) the Reserve Sector of the replacement disk drive before will accept it and rebuild to it. See Chapter 5 of the Product Manual for instructions.

Synchronize Disk Array

When I try to Synchronize the Disk Array, I receive an error message.

If another operation, such as Initializing or Rebuilding is in progress, you cannot perform a Synchronization at the same time. Rather than doing a manual Synchronization, consider automating. See the Subsystem Scheduler tab.

Migrate Disk Array

When I try to migrate a disk array from one RAID level to another, why doesn't the controller let me do it?

Most RAID levels have a minimum and maximum number of disk drives. Be sure you have the correct number of drives available for the target disk array. See RAID Conversion and RAID Levels.

Only a single-drive RAID 0 can migrate to a RAID 1 disk array.

I want to add two more drives to my RAID 1 disk array. Why won't the controller let me migrate it?

A RAID 1 disk array uses only two disk drives. In this case, you can add two physical drives, then convert to a RAID 10 disk array. Most RAID levels have a minimum and maximum number of disk drives. See RAID Levels.

Media Patrol and PDM

Media Patrol and PDM are enabled on my system. But they never report anything.

This is a good sign. Media Patrol and PDM operate transparently until problems are found on a physical drive.
